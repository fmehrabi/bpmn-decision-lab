# Question Authoring Guide

## Required design template

Every new item should state:

1. **Learning objective:** what observable process skill is being assessed?
2. **Competency:** which diagnostic group receives the score?
3. **Difficulty:** introductory, intermediate, or advanced.
4. **Evidence:** what diagram fragment, metric, or operational fact is necessary?
5. **Stem:** what decision or inference is required?
6. **Options:** four plausible choices of comparable specificity.
7. **Score:** 100 for the preferred choice, with partial credit only when an option is genuinely defensible but incomplete.
8. **Feedback:** why the option does or does not fit the stated conditions.
9. **Impact:** plausible process consequence if the organisation followed the option.
10. **Learning tip:** what principle should the learner carry forward?

## Quality checklist

- Is the question self-contained without relying on a previous screen?
- Is every required number, unit, condition, and constraint stated?
- Is exactly one option clearly the highest-scored under the case assumptions?
- Are distractors realistic mistakes in process reasoning, not silly choices?
- Could more than one option be defensible? If so, state the objective and constraints more precisely or use a ranking/multi-select format in a future version.
- Does the feedback explain the actual option, not merely label it right or wrong?
- Does the consequence explain an operational or organisational effect?
- Does the item test reasoning rather than a wording trick?
- Is the question aligned with the BPMN model and simulation assumptions?
- Does the item avoid claims unsupported by the evidence?

## Image and diagram questions

Use a process fragment, lane, gateway, exception path, resource map, or quantitative chart that is legible at ordinary laptop width. A diagram should contain only the elements needed to answer the item. Keep a text description in the item prompt or evidence caption so that the learning target is not inaccessible to a learner who cannot interpret the visual.

## Consequence quality

Consequences should distinguish:
- direct flow effect (routing, rework, or control bypass);
- operational effect (queue, cycle time, throughput, or utilisation);
- economic effect (labour, operating cost, or downstream cost);
- quality/risk effect (escape defects, service failure, or compliance exposure).

Do not attach made-up numerical effects to an answer unless the simulation model actually computes them and the assumptions are disclosed.

## Scoring principles

- Use full credit for a strong answer under the case's stated objectives.
- Use partial credit for an option that recognises part of the issue but misses evidence, constraints, or sequencing.
- Use zero for choices that violate a critical condition or introduce a major process risk without justification.
- Weight items only when their contribution to the learning outcome warrants it.
- Do not use the resulting score as a validated measure of a stable personal trait.

## Reviewing a question before release

The item author should independently solve the item, check all options against the exact stem, calculate any quantitative answer separately, verify every impact, and ask another instructor to review ambiguous or contestable choices.
